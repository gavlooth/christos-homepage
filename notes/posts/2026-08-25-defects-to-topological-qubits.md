I have been trying to write down, from scratch, whether a lattice of real crystal defects could host the kind of topological protection people usually reserve for toy Hamiltonians. The result is a book-length draft:

[Defects to Topological Qubits (PDF)](/notes/assets/defects-to-topological-qubits.pdf)

It starts with a Stern–Gerlach magnet and ends with a grade. In between: color centers, coupling, anyons, string nets, and a fabrication budget that does not flinch.

The short version of the plot is three distinctions I kept having to re-earn:

1. A two-level atom is not yet a computer.
2. A simulation of a strange particle is not a material that contains one.
3. A long-lived spin is not automatically a protected logical bit.

The Fibonacci-quasicrystal note on this site was a sketch. This is the longer argument that sketch was pointing at. It is still a draft. The PDF is the thing to read.
